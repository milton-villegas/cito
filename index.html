Patient: John Doe, born 14 March 1958. Patient ID 104582.

Admission: 10 September 2026. Discharge: 14 September 2026.

Diagnoses

1. Acute exacerbation of COPD; GOLD 2 airflow obstruction.

Most likely triggered by viral respiratory tract infection.

No evidence of pneumonia.

Current smoker, approximately 40 pack-years.

2. Arterial hypertension.

History

Four days of progressive dyspnoea, increased cough, yellowish sputum and reduced exercise tolerance.

COPD diagnosed in 2021.

Most recent outpatient spirometry: FEV1 1.75 L (58% predicted), FVC 3.42 L (88% predicted), FEV1/FVC 0.51.

He has had one previous moderate COPD exacerbation during the past 12 months without hospitalisation.

No long-term oxygen therapy.

Examination on admission

Alert and oriented; moderately dyspnoeic at rest.

SpO₂ 88% on room air; 92% with 2 L/min oxygen.

Bilateral expiratory wheeze; no peripheral oedema.

Investigations

Respiratory viral PCR positive for rhinovirus; SARS-CoV-2 and influenza PCR negative.

Chest X-ray: no evidence of pneumonia.

Arterial blood gas: no significant respiratory acidosis.

Clinical course

Supplemental oxygen discontinued on day three.

At discharge: SpO₂ 92–94% on room air.

Independently mobile; stable general condition.

Medication at discharge

New or changed:

Tiotropium/olodaterol inhaler — 2 inhalations once daily.

Salbutamol inhaler — 1–2 inhalations as required for dyspnoea.

Prednisone 40 mg once daily — complete a total course of 5 days.

Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid 875/125 mg twice daily — complete a total course of 5 days.

Pre-existing:

Amlodipine 5 mg once daily.

Recommendations

GP follow-up within 7–14 days.

Reassess respiratory symptoms, exercise tolerance and oxygen saturation.

Review smoking status and inhaler technique.

Repeat pulmonary function testing in approximately 4–8 weeks.

Review maintenance therapy and vaccination status.

Consider pulmonary rehabilitation if limitations persist.

Attending physician, Department of Internal Medicine

The GP uses the reviewed overview to define and coordinate the patient’s aftercare plan outside AirBridge.

AirBridge organizes authorized COPD documents available in a GP practice, together with a hospital discharge report, into one structured, chronological and source-linked overview. The GP reviews the information and remains responsible for every clinical and aftercare decision.

AirBridge does not diagnose, prescribe, recommend treatment, select medication, calculate risk or choose the aftercare plan.
